Notes:

- Silhouette: Long low wedge with a front-only nose slot, filled roof and cabin, larger nose drum and smaller tail drum, and a squared louvered tail. Top plan matches the two-cheek drawing. Side shoulder and canopy are simpler and a little taller than the still, and the cheek blades run farther ahead of the drums than the museum front.
- Surface: Gullwing reads as a diagonal shadowed seam on the flank in the close-ups and as a thin dark cut at sheet size. Rear louvers, roof panel breaks, and the open nose slot all read. Drum faces are flat covered housings with no rim, slots, or glow; they are smoother and blanker than the prop. Wear is a fine speckle rather than edge grime.
- Lighting: White cheek strips, a red tail bar, small amber side markers, and a low red/blue roof bar are all present. Cool light sits in the nose channel and under the skirt. The rim row picks out the wedge, drums, and slot. The channel bar is hotter than the stills.
- Material: Night reads as gunmetal with darker panel breaks, speckled dirt, and a tinted reflective canopy. Studio shows clear-coat highlights over a dark lower body. It is not a one-tone plastic shell, but the breakup is even and the glass is cleaner than the filmed prop.
- Film: At street distance in the night frames the wedge, drums, slot, and lamps would sit next to the stills. A hero close-up would show the faceted body, the shallow door cut, and the block POLICE plaque as a real-time asset.
- Reads as K: Immediate from the split nose, covered drums, POLICE flank, low light bar, and louvered tail. It does not need a caption. It is a cleaned-up reading of the prop, not a near-still match.
- Stray parts: No. Nothing reads as a fin, a rod, a loose panel, or a part bolted on past the body.
