## What the film is asking these two to do

Published accounts, used as reference. No frames are in this commit. The longer citation is in the B1 critique.

- Dennis Gassner, one word from Denis Villeneuve: brutality. The pattern language starts with the angular spinner and carries into the buildings. Scale is the power. Sunlight in the film belongs to Wallace. ([IndieWire, 23 Oct 2017](https://www.indiewire.com/awards/industry/blade-runner-2049-dystopia-production-design-denis-villeneuve-1201891427/); [Deadline, 15 Feb 2018](https://deadline.com/2018/02/blade-runner-2049-dennis-gassner-oscars-production-design-interview-1202280798/))
- John Nelson: one packed carpet, Montreal on a bad February day, then a leap of scales. Brutalist masses, heavy tops, signs restrained. The LAPD approach miniature is 1/48 and about 15 ft tall. At 4.5 m that is 216 m. ([vfxblog, 26 Oct 2017](https://vfxblog.com/2017/10/26/lets-move-to-la-in-2049/))
- Wētā Workshop: the buildings are lampshades. LED inside, foam and tape killing the windows that should stay dark, parallax rooms only on a face a flyby could enter. Alex Funke shot them in smoke, lights as separate passes. ([Wētā Workshop](https://www.wetaworkshop.com/projects/bladerunner-2049); [ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/weta-workshop-blade-runner-2049/))

The lantern's job is a dark slab whose cross reads from the sidewalk and from the fly, without becoming a smaller MT-4. MT-4 is already a cruciform plan with a warm glass lantern crown (`megatower-4` in `src/districts/financial-megatowers/specs.ts`). The buttress's job is one mass: a windowed core and corner piers that stay attached, without becoming MT-1's raking buttresses or MT-7's landmark cage crown. Both stay in the fabric band, under the inversion, which is centred at 150–320 m (`docs/BIBLE.md`).

## What works

Keep these. A later pass that puts a glow crown back on either family, lights the buttress like a hotel, or lifts the fabric through 320 m will leave the film.

- **The stand-in column is the picture this replaced.** The lantern street before the merge is a dark wall, a door, and a sign. The buttress street before the merge is a blank monolith with one warm speck. The after frames are a cross and a floor grid. That was the assignment, and the street and fly frames meet it.
- **The lantern cross is a street figure.** On the worked footprint the street wing is 34 m wide and 14 m deep. The slit is 2.4 m wide, starts one storey (3.6 m) above the ground, and runs 190.8 m, to nine tenths of the height. A 12.5 m lip crosses it at 5.05 m, beside the door (the door centre is 4.4 m off the wing centre; the slit is another 5.6 m over). The street frame shows that cross, the `NINTH RING` lease, the figure, and the flight. B1's test was whether this survived a sidewalk camera. It does.
- **The mid bar is stone.** The belt is 6.2 m of `Style.Solid` at 0.52 of the height, with a 1.55 m lip on it. The fly frame, aimed at that height, shows a dark shaft, a segmented light column, and one bar. A 2 m stripe would have vanished. 6.2 m is the width that holds.
- **The buttress is one building from the fly.** Core 41.6 × 29.1 m, `Style.Megablock`, so the cell is a 3.6 m storey. Corner piers 8 × 8 m, up to 0.64 of the height (138 m here), set out at the lot corners with an 11 m gap back to the core. Ties are 1.45 m plates at 0.32 and 0.50 of the height, both well below the pier tops, so the recipe keeps both. The fly frame shows the core, the separated piers, the slit slots on those piers, and the plates that tie them. MT-1 keeps the rakers. This is corner piers.
- **No glow crown.** `dressRoof` skips the lantern. The buttress roof is a pad, four posts, a ring, and a mast off the crown. The monolith keeps the small lamp hat. The sky frames have no second hat.
- **Human metres, shared with the rest of the carpet.** `PERSON_M` is 1.80. The opening is 2.2 m square, so the headroom is 0.40 m. `planFlight(3.6)` is 21 risers at 0.171 m, tread 0.29 m, run 6.09 m. The rail is 1.05 m. The lantern brow is 2.4 × 1.05 × 0.26 m at 2.42 m. The buttress brow is 2.3 × 1.15 × 0.28 m at 2.45 m. Both sit just above the door head. The pale plate behind the lantern treads is 0.14 m thick and 3.85 m tall, which is why the risers read on a dark wall. The street frames show a figure that fits the opening and a flight of real steps. The spinner in the fly frames is a vehicle. It is not a scale cue.
- **Glow stays a column or a bar.** `Style.Glow` cells are 6.0 × 4.5 m (`facade.ts`). A face taller and wider than that tiles into a window wall. The slit is 2.4 m wide, one column. The lips and the pier caps are 1.55 m tall, one row. The door plate buries the 0.63 m sill (the pane starts at 0.14 of the 4.5 m cell) so the 2.2 m opening is the lit part. That discipline is what lets the cross stay a cross.
- **Seeds and the memory budget.** The roll is unchanged. `r.chance(0.35)` for the lantern's rear sign is still in that place in `placeLot`. The B2 note counts 42 lanterns and 59 buttresses downtown. Metres do not change with the tier. Low keeps the masses, including the cage and the parapet. Textures added: 0. No punctual light.

## What fails

### The buttress street is a lit chart

The core is `lit: 0.86`, hardcoded. Night `windowLit` is 1, and the shader keeps 0.45 of that, so about 39% of the bays burn. A megablock pane is about 2.0 × 1.6 m. The street frame is that grid from the top of the crop down to the door: bright squares, mullions, rain in front of them. Wētā taped the lampshade and left a few rooms. This wall leaves most of the tape off.

The lot's own `lit` is ignored. Financial lots roll 0.28–0.52, DTLA wide lots 0.22–0.48, Long Beach 0.07–0.18. A coastal buttress burns at the same 0.86 as a downtown one. The secondary core was the quiet dial. The family overrides it.

The unlit megablock cell is already a punched hole (the shader darkens glass that lost the lottery). The floor grid the fly needed does not require two fifths of the panes to be on. At the lot's 0.40, the lit fraction is about 0.18. The street becomes a dark wall with scattered rooms, and the fly still has a storey rhythm.

### The street piers miss the sidewalk camera

On the worked footprint the buttress base piers sit at ±17.5 m from the door, 0.72 m wide, projecting 0.55 m, and only when that offset clears 8.6 m. The street camera is 10.5 m out, on the opening. The piers are down the block. The frame shows the grid, the door, and the stair, and no pilaster.

The lantern does the placement the right way: up to four piers at 2.7 m, starting just past the flight, projecting about 1.2 m, two storeys tall. They are `Style.Solid` at `lit` 0.08, the same dark as the slab, so they vanish in the street frame anyway. The plate behind the treads is doing the work the piers were drawn to do.

The brows are the right size and the right height. At 0.26–0.28 m of dark stone over a lit door, they drop out of these frames. The metres can stay. They need a lighter face if the sidewalk is meant to catch them.

### The cross is a decal, and the roofs flatten

At street distance the slit is a stack of glow cells, about 42 on a 216 m shaft, every one lit. Glow ignores the window lottery. `glowLevel` at night is `lit × 1.45`, and the shell is `NITS.window` (18). The slit at `lit` 0.76 is on the order of 24 nits in the pane and about 16 nits once the face is small. Bloom starts at 32 nits. The fly frame shows sharp rectangles and rain that does not catch the colour. L1 already scored that limit for signs. A lantern shaft that is fully on will read as a fixture, which is what makes the cross visible, and it will not throw light into the haze.

Each lip is 1.55 m tall and up to 12.5 m wide. Height stays inside one 4.5 m cell. Width crosses two 6 m cells, so the bar is two bays with a thin joint. Because the lip's own UV starts at 0, the bottom 0.63 m falls in the cell sill and stays dark at street distance. The lit part of the authored 1.55 m bar is about 0.9 m. The door plate avoids this by burying that sill. The cross and the pier caps do not.

The three distances show three different pieces, which is right, and the piece that was supposed to be the roof is the one that drops out.

- Street: slit base and the lip at 5 m.
- Fly: the belt. The script starts 52 m out and 8 m off the slit, so the pose is nearly head-on. The depth arm (10 × 30 m on the worked footprint) sits behind the 14 m wing, and the plan cross stays hidden.
- Sky: the camera is at 0.74 of the height, aimed at 0.86, so it gets the upper lip and the top of the slit. The parapet is 1.35 m. On that frame the roof is a flat dark edge. The mast is 8 m, detail 1, with a 0.45 m obstruction lamp. It does not carry a silhouette.

The buttress sky camera is at the same fraction of the height. The piers stop at 0.64, below the aim, so the skyline of the buttress is the core alone. The cage is a 1.05 m pad, posts of 8.5 m (6.5 m on variant 1), and a ring 0.7 m tall by 0.45 m thick. The sky frame shows two short lumps and a thin lip, plus the offset mast. MT-7 already owns a cage crown on a landmark shaft. A fabric cage has to be legible as a roof room at this camera, or the family loses its skyline the way the monolith kept only its hat.

`dressRoof` still runs for the buttress. It places that box at `d.height`, and by then the mast tip is the tallest point, about 13.5 m above the roof on variant 0, so the box sits up at the obstruction lamp. The cage already has two plants and three antennas at detail 2. The extra box competes with the ring.

Forty-odd lanterns with the belt at the same 0.52, the upper lip at the same 0.78, and the slit at the same 2.4 m will read as one logo. Variants change the wing from 34 m to 40 m and the fin count. They do not move the cross.

### What this does to the carpet

B1's wide-lot table was one monolith in three, because the lantern and the buttress were rebuilt as that monolith after the roll. They are now one eighth each. The dark wedge is no longer the default. A downtown skyline gains crosses and piered blocks among the band slabs, stacks, and cantilevers. That separation is the gain.

The remaining rhyme: the lantern belt is the band slab's 6.2 m stripe, and the lips are the same 1.55 m the band slab uses. From a distance where the slit is a scratch, a lantern with the belt in frame is a cousin of the band slab. The buttress at 0.86 lit is the bright object in a field the B1 critique described as black. One eighth of the wide lots, at that brightness, shifts the night.

A pier at about 1.4 m wide, projecting about 1.2 m, two storeys tall, clear of that run, is what the 10.5 m camera can see.
